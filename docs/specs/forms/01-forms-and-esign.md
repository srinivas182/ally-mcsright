# Forms 01 — Native forms and e-signature (Team B, 5 sprints)

Status: **Specification approved** (owner approved all seven additions after the leader review of dotloop,
DocuSign Transaction Rooms with Lone Wolf zipForm, SkySlope Forms with DigiSign, and Lone Wolf Transactions with
Authentisign). Version 1.0 stays at Sprint 63.

## Parity with the leaders

State and association forms library (licensed; libraries plug in as licences arrive — until then brokerages use
their own forms and any uploaded PDF), auto-fill from the deal (parties, property, price, dates, agents,
brokerage), clause library, brokerage templates, multi-signer e-signature with signing order, initials and date
fields, mobile and in-person signing, reminders, ESIGN/UETA consent to electronic records, audit certificate,
counter-offers and addenda, re-signing after changes, and completed documents filed to the deal's checklist.

## Better than the leaders (approved)

1. **Pre-send check**: missing signatures, initials, dates or blank required fields are flagged before sending.
2. **Re-sign only what changed**: signers see exactly what changed and re-sign only those parts.
3. **Tamper-evident sealing**: completed documents are sealed with a SHA-256 fingerprint and a digital signature
   anyone can verify.
4. **Wire-fraud protection**: every signing email carries the warning; forms never carry wiring instructions
   (bank, routing or account details are blocked from form fields).
5. **Signer verification**: an SMS code for everyday documents; ID verification for high-risk ones.
6. **Smart counter-offers**: generated from the original with a summary of changed terms, linked in one history.
7. **Plain-English summaries** of each form for clients, marked as not legal advice.

## Technical notes

PDFs are normalised with `qpdf` (object streams disabled, PDF 1.4-compatible output) so FPDI can import any
uploaded form; FPDI + FPDF stamp values, signatures and the audit certificate. Field positions are stored in PDF
points from the page's top-left. Bindings read the deal, its parties, its agent and the brokerage.

## Delivery

- **Part 1 (Sprint 14B)**: templates (upload, normalise, fields with type, page, position, signer role, binding,
  required), auto-fill from the deal, clause library, packets on a deal (forms, values, signer roles), filled
  PDF preview, pre-send check, wiring-instructions guard.
- **Part 2 (delivered, Sprint 14B-2)**: send after the pre-send check passes; signers emailed in signing order
  (everyone with the lowest unsigned order) with a secret link and the **wire-fraud warning in every email**;
  ESIGN/UETA disclosure and consent before signing; optional **text-code verification** (Core `SmsSender`: Twilio
  when configured, otherwise logged; five tries); sign by **typing the exact name or drawing**, plus initials;
  decline with a reason (the agent is emailed); void (links stop working); reminders every 2 days, up to 3, each
  with a fresh link; **in-person signing** on the agent's device for whoever is next; completion assembles one PDF
  (values, signatures, initials, signing dates) with a **signature certificate** page (signers, consent, IPs,
  verification, every event), **seals** it (SHA-256 signed with Ed25519; key from FORMS_SEAL_SEED or derived from
  the app key), files it to the deal's checklist items named by the forms (into the review queue), and emails
  every signer their copy; anyone can **verify a copy** at /verify-document. PDFs are read as uploaded (FPDI reads
  compressed PDFs); qpdf only repairs ones FPDI cannot read.
- **Part 3 (delivered, Sprint 14B-3)**: visual field placement — each page is rendered on the server with `pdftoppm`
  at 72 dpi (1 pixel = 1 PDF point, cached by the PDF's fingerprint); staff drag on the page to draw a field (a
  click places a standard one), drag to move, use the corner to resize, nudge with the arrow keys (Shift = 10 pt),
  Delete to remove, Ctrl/Cmd+D to duplicate; a side panel sets label, key, type, signer and auto-fill source; boxes
  are coloured by type and stay inside the page; the coordinate table remains as a fallback (and opens
  automatically if the renderer is unavailable). Sealing: a **dedicated key** (`FORMS_SEAL_SEED`, generated by
  `php artisan forms:seal-key`; a critical log in production when missing) and **key rotation** — old public keys in
  `FORMS_SEAL_PREVIOUS_PUBLIC_KEYS` keep past seals verifiable. `poppler-utils` added to the Docker image.
- **Part 4** (delivered): **re-sign only what changed** — while a packet is out for signature the agent can change
  fillable terms with a reason (wire-instruction guard applies). Signers who already signed get an email listing
  each change (`Label: old → new`) and a fresh link where they initial only those changes (or decline them);
  signers who have not yet signed simply sign the new terms. The packet completes only when everyone has signed
  **and** every change is initialled; the certificate lists each change, its reason and who initialled it, when
  and from where. **Smart counter-offers** — from a sent, declined or signed packet, pick the terms to change; a new
  draft is generated with the same forms, template versions and signers, titled "… — counter N", carrying a
  summary of changed terms; an open original is voided ("Countered in round N"); only one open counter or
  addendum per packet. **Addenda** — chosen forms attached to a fully signed packet with the same signers. All
  packets of one negotiation are linked (`negotiation_ulid`, `round`, `kind`, `parent_packet_id`) and shown as one
  history on the packet page; the deal's forms list marks counters and addenda. New tables: `form_changes`,
  `form_change_acks`.
- **Part 5** (leader review: DocuSign Identify, Adobe Acrobat Sign, Lone Wolf zipForm/Authentisign, dotloop,
  SkySlope, CertifID; ten additions approved), delivered in three sprints:
    - **5a — identity checks** (delivered): signers prove who they are with a **government ID and a live selfie**
      (Stripe Identity on the brokerage's own Stripe account; provider-agnostic, `FORMS_IDENTITY_DRIVER`). Who must
      verify follows the brokerage policy — _when there are fraud signals_ (default), _every seller_, _every signer_
      or _off_; agents never verify; forms marked **high-risk** (deeds, powers of attorney) make every client signer
      verify. **Seller-impersonation signals** are computed per packet and shown on the packet page: vacant land,
      owner not living at the property, owner's mailing address out of state, price below a set % of the estimated
      value, seller not met in person, and seller not named as an **owner of record** (property-data provider; one
      lookup per packet; until a provider is connected no ownership signals are raised). The verified name must
      match the signer (first and last name, accents and middle names tolerated); a seller must also match the owner
      of record, otherwise signing pauses, the agent is alerted, and only the broker or office staff can accept it
      with a written reason (audited). Checks are **reused for 12 months** for the same email and name. RightAlly
      keeps only the outcome (name, document type and country, dates) — **never ID images**. The certificate records
      each verification and the owner of record (and any accepted mismatch). Sending is blocked when checks are
      needed but no identity service is set up. Knowledge-based questions are deliberately not offered.
    - **5b — plain-English summaries** (delivered): AI writes a packet summary from the **filled-in terms only**
      (sent to the model as untrusted data through the AI gateway: redaction, output policy, credits) — headline,
      points by topic, deadlines, "what you're agreeing to", and terms worth a second look — in **English and
      Spanish**. Code then checks it: every amount and every date in a line must appear in the documents, or the
      line is dropped (the agent sees how many); a deadline date not written in the documents is cleared to "date to
      set"; every date field is always a deadline. The disclaimer ("not legal advice; the documents you sign are
      what count") is added in code in both languages. The summary is stored encrypted, tied to a hash of the terms:
      the **agent approves** it before signers see it, and any later change (including a change after sending) makes
      it stale and hidden until written and approved again. **Counter-offer differences** are spelled out exactly by
      code, not AI ("$485,000 → $470,000 (down $15,000)", "07/01 → 07/11 (10 days later)") in counters, change
      emails and history. **Deadlines → key dates**: the agent picks deadlines (setting any missing dates) and adds
      them to the deal's **Key dates** (new on the deal page: add by hand, tick off, remove); `transactions:date-
        reminders` emails the agent daily about dates due in 2 days, today, or missed yesterday (once a day; never
      for ticked-off dates or closed deals) — Deal-IQ escalation builds on these. **Clause check** before sending:
      typed special terms (text of 60+ characters) that are not clause-library wording are listed on the packet;
      the brokerage chooses _warn_ (default), _need broker approval before sending_, or _off_; approval is tied to
      the exact wording, so an edit needs a new approval. Pure text comparison, no AI.
    - **5c — association form libraries**: connector framework, NRDS membership check, new-version alerts with a
      side-by-side of changes, association PDF package import, template import from dotloop, SkySlope and zipForm.
        - Later: remote online notarization (partner) for deeds and powers of attorney.
